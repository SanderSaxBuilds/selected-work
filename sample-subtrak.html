Three posts about the small things that slow a job down.

Each post takes one everyday problem on a job, a repeated process, a vague handoff or an unclear finish, and turns it into one thing the team can try this week.

The posts

Product statements stick to features shown on the public Subtrak website: reusable processes, task owners and timing, attached instructions, reminders and completion evidence. Switch each post between its LinkedIn and short-form copy, and copy either one as plain text.

01
Process

Pick one repeated process

Before you document everything, choose one process that keeps coming back. A project handoff. A material order. A closeout check.

Write the steps and name the owner. Try the sequence on one job, then review what still needed explanation.

Subtrak supports reusable processes with tasks, responsibility and timing. Which repeated process would you start with?

One repeated process is enough to begin. Write the steps, name the owner and review what the team needs next.

02
Handoff

Give the handoff a clear next step

“Can you take care of it?” leaves a lot to interpret.

A useful handoff makes the next step specific: who is responsible, when it is needed and where the instructions live.

Subtrak lets tasks carry instructions and reminders. Pick one handoff and check whether the person receiving it can act without another round of questions.

Who owns it? When is it due? What is needed?

Three useful questions for the next handoff. Keep the instructions close to the task.

03
Closeout

Agree what complete means

A status update is more useful when everyone knows what it means.

For an important step, agree the final check before work begins. That might be a photo, a file or a review by the person responsible for the next stage.

Subtrak supports completion evidence alongside tasks. What would make the next handoff clearer on your current job?

Make complete visible. Agree the final check, attach the relevant proof and make the next step clear.

What a monthly engagement would need

From the team

  • Weekly volume of posts and clips
  • Approved product facts and brand assets
  • Founder recordings, if clips are part of the plan
  • Who approves copy, and who publishes and replies

What this kit does not include

  • Product footage or screen recordings
  • Audio, voiceover or recorded interviews
  • Published posts or account access
  • Results from earlier SaaS social campaigns
