A printed poster is four thin layers of ink: cyan, magenta, yellow and black. Pull the plates apart, tilt the stack, switch inks off, knock the registration out, then put a loupe on the proof and count the dots.

01 / Separate

Pull the plates apart

Keep scrolling. Your position here sets how far apart the four plates float, from one printed sheet to a stack of films.

Your device asks for reduced motion, so scrolling leaves the plates still. The slider below does the same job.

02 / Tilt

Look along the stack

Move the pointer across the booth. The angle follows within a small range and settles back when you leave. Each film carries only its own ink, so the red sun is magenta on one plate and yellow on another.

03 / Plates

Switch inks off

Take a plate away and see what it was doing. Without yellow the sun turns pink, without cyan the sea goes purple and the sky disappears. The proof desk further down follows these switches too.

04 / Registration

Knock it out of register

On press, each plate has to land on the same spot. The plates settle back into one sheet here. Drag the slider and the cyan and magenta plates drift, which shows up first as coloured edges on the sun, the corner marks and the headline at the top of this page.

Measured as if the poster were printed on an A3 sheet.

05 / Proof desk

Put a loupe on it

Up close, a printed colour is not a colour at all. Each ink is a field of dots set at its own angle, and the dots grow where the ink gets heavier. Drag the ring over the proof, or focus the proof and use the arrow keys.

Four SVG files, one variable, one canvas

Each plate is a small SVG drawn in a single ink. Flat, they sit on a white sheet with mix-blend-mode: multiply, which is how inks mix on paper. Separated, the same drawings move onto films in a 3D stack, spaced by a CSS custom property fed into translateZ.

For the loupe, each plate is drawn once more in plain black on a hidden canvas and read back as a map of ink density. The loupe turns that map into round dots at the classic screen angles, cyan at 15 degrees, magenta at 75, yellow at 0 and black at 45, and multiplies them together. No libraries.

for (const [p, angle] of SCREENS) {
  const d = density[p];                  // 0 to 1, from the plate drawing
  forEachCell(angle, cell, (x, y) => {
    const r = cell * Math.sqrt(d(x, y) / Math.PI);
    if (r > .15) dot(x, y, r, INK[p]);   // multiplied onto white paper
  });
}
